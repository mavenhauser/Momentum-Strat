Precursors (no per-ticker data retained)

P0 — pattern-based, abandoned Entry required a "big base near highs," ATR-based "compression," a "fresh breakout," market cap > $2B, and EMA8/21/50 all below price. Produced zero trades on AMD across the full history: compression (tight ATR) and a fresh breakout are close to mutually exclusive on the same bar, since a breakout day naturally expands ATR. Abandoned once it was confirmed the base/compression/breakout criteria were visual judgment calls, not something reliable to encode as a formula.
P1 — percent-based trim, multi-day hold SMA200 + EMA + PMH/PML entry, -5% stop, trim 25% at +25% price move, another 25% at +50%, breakeven stop after each trim. Exact per-ticker figures weren't retained, but net P&L came in far below simple buy-and-hold on the same names despite genuine rallies in the underlying stocks — traced mainly to 20%-of-equity sizing diluting gains and to trim levels set as arbitrary price percentages rather than a risk-defined multiple. This directly motivated the switch to R-multiple-based trims and a fixed swing exit, i.e. Variant A.

Why this is the pick: the live scanner polling every 10 minutes to match G's continuous-checking backtest is expensive — this tests whether a much cheaper hourly check can hold up. Checking once an hour with a 6-candle (trailing-hour) lookback, rather than a naive single check that only inspects the current bar, gets a result that's nearly indistinguishable from G's (+10.79% vs. G's +10.39% avg, 73.0% vs. 70.5% win rate, 31 vs. 33 trades) at roughly 1/5th the checks. Two design traps were caught and fixed along the way: a single fixed check-time (e.g. "run once at 1:50pm") means buying at a stale, already-moved price rather than the actual breakout price — not a real signal; and an early "latch since 10am" version stayed true all day, causing bogus same-day re-entries after a stop-out even with no fresh breakout (that version hit +23.45% avg, but it was inflated by the bug, not real edge). Bounding the lookback to a rolling 6 candles fixed both problems.

Position sizing: 20% vs. 100% of equity (Variant H)

Every backtest in this record already runs each ticker independently — DELL, AMD, NVDA, MU, and TSLA each get their own fresh $100k, never a shared or split pool. So "20% position size" was never a 1-of-5 portfolio allocation; it's per-trade sizing within one ticker's own account, originally set low to avoid margin calls on multi-day/short holds. Since H is long-only, that constraint no longer applies — retested at 100% to see the strategy's return with no artificial capital cap.

Downside stress test: does this depend on an exceptional bull run?

The R:R sweep's returns (up to +222% on MU) raised an obvious question: how much of that is genuine edge vs. these 5 tickers simply having an extraordinary run during the tested window? The direct test — rerunning H on the same 5 tickers over an earlier, rougher stretch — turned out to be impossible: TradingView's 10-minute intraday history for these symbols is hard-capped at Aug/Sep 2025 (confirmed via the Strategy Tester's internal dateRange.backtest metadata, identical across MU/AMD/NVDA/TSLA and nearly identical for DELL), and panning/scrolling the chart does not load anything earlier. There is no way to test the exact intraday strategy before that date.

Day-trade gap strategy (separate from Variant H, exploratory)

A distinct strategy concept, not a variant of H: same-day exit only, designed specifically around gap-day behavior (a stock can gap up premarket and still open weak and fade, or gap up and keep extending — the strategy needs to read which one is happening, not just assume continuation).

Attempt 1 — opening-range breakout/breakdown, rejected Gap filter (≥5% from prior close) + a ~20-minute opening range (9:30/9:40 bars on the 10-min chart) as the long/short trigger, stop at the opposite side of the range, forced flat at EOD. Tested on WULF, KLAC, AEHR (gap-ups) and RIVN, IONS, FRMI (gap-downs): 4 of 6 lost money, 2 produced zero trades. Two real problems found, not just weak performance: (1) the fade/reversal trade essentially never fired — every trade was a continuation in the original gap direction, meaning the setup never caught the "gaps up, opens weak, sells off" pattern it was built for; (2) RIVN (-11.17%) and IONS (-20.94%) — the two biggest reported gaps in the sample — produced zero trades, traced to a timing mismatch between the premarket scanner's snapshot-based gap % (captured before the bell) and the script's regular-session-open-based gap % (can diverge if the stock keeps moving between the premarket snapshot and 9:30).
Attempt 2 — "infused" into Variant H as a second entry pathway, reverted Kept H's swing-long entries exactly as-is, and added a new day-trade short pathway on top: any day with a ≥5% gap (either direction) where price then breaks below premarket low → short, stop at premarket high, forced flat at EOD. Tested on the 5 new tickers (AVGO, PLTR, MSFT, AAPL, GOOGL): the short pathway barely fired at all (1 trade out of 16 total, on PLTR) and the combined average (-0.20%) was worse than H alone. Reverted back to pure Variant H for two reasons: (1) the short pathway added complexity without adding much signal for this ticker set, and (2) a real logic flaw was identified — the same rule fires shorts on gap-downs that break even lower, which fights the well-known "gap fill" tendency (gap-downs statistically tend to bounce/mean-revert back toward prior close, not keep falling) rather than riding it.
Attempt 3 — gap-down long (mean-reversion), next up Directly motivated by the gap-fill critique above: instead of fighting the reversion tendency, trade with it. If a stock gaps down ≥5%, go long once the market opens (no confirmation trigger — first regular-session bar) and see if the bounce/fill has real edge. Exit: stop-loss + forced flat at EOD, consistent with the day-trade-only principle this whole thread started from. Not yet tested.

Premarket gappers scanner: low count diagnosis

Investigated why a scan day returned only 2 gappers. Confirmed via a raw re-fetch of the top 20 premarket movers by gap%: only 2 (FBRX $1.56B, TRAX $1.5B) cleared the scanner's $1B market cap filter — the other 18, including movers as large as +490%, were mostly microcap/nanocap names (several under $10M market cap). Not a bug — the filter is working as designed; today just had few large-cap gappers. Offered to lower the market cap floor to surface more names; explicitly declined ("keep to as it is for now") — filter left unchanged. Live trading plan: options-based swing, IBKR paper account

Important caveat, still true: every result above is a stock-position backtest. TradingView's Strategy Tester does not model options at all — no strike/expiry selection, no theta decay, no IV changes, no bid-ask spread cost, no assignment mechanics. The win rate / avg win / avg loss table is the closest available proxy (underlying price-move terms), but actual option P&L depends on strike, DTE, and IV changes not modeled here.

Exit: systematic ceiling + discretionary overlay

The stock-level exit rules (-10% stop, 2R trim to breakeven, 20-trading-day time exit) act as a ceiling, not a mandate — real exits are discretionary and typically earlier:

Discretionary exit rule If theta decay exceeds delta's dollar contribution and there's no catalyst on the horizon, exit regardless of where the stock-level stop/trim would say. This compensates for exactly what the stock backtest can't see: option-specific time-decay risk.
Continuation rule Only hold into the "let the multi-day/week move play out" phase once the option is already ITM. If it never gets there, exit — don't wait around hoping the stock catches up before theta erodes the position.

Build status: option picker + enriched Telegram alert — working end-to-end

momentum_option_picker.py selects the nearest monthly OpEx ≥14 days out and picks the cheapest contract clearing delta>0.25 / volume>500 / OI>1000, sized to 1% of IBKR paper account NLV. Wired into momentum_strategy_scanner.py — every new Telegram hit includes a suggested contract line. Never places an order — no order-placement code exists in any of the three files, verified by inspection. Confirmed live end-to-end: NVDA → 2026-08-21 210C, delta 0.29, IV 38%, vol 13,603, OI 45,070, bid $3.65/ask $3.80, 28 contracts suggested against a $1,073,124 SGD account.

Data source switched from IBKR to TastyTrade after diagnosing why IBKR was unreliable: IBKR connection/account-NLV/chain-structure all worked consistently, but per-contract data (delta/IV/volume/OI/bid-ask) came back empty on 3 of 4 attempts (IBKR error 10091, "additional subscription required"). Reused the existing SPX Scalp project's TastyTrade OAuth credentials (copied into an isolated ~/.trading-scanners/tastytrade.env so the two projects stay independent) via a new momentum_tastytrade_data.py module. Found the same class of gap there too at first — TastyTrade's REST market-data snapshot (get_market_data_by_type, the same call the SPX client uses successfully for index options) returned zero rows for real, liquid equity-option strikes. Fix: pulled everything (greeks, open interest, bid/ask, volume) from TastyTrade's DXLink streaming API instead of the REST snapshot — confirmed reliable across a full options chain in testing. IBKR is now used only for account NLV (the actual paper account being sized against), nothing else.
