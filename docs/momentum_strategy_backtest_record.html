Precursors (no per-ticker data retained)

P0 — pattern-based, abandoned Entry required a "big base near highs," ATR-based "compression," a "fresh breakout," market cap > $2B, and EMA8/21/50 all below price. Produced zero trades on AMD across the full history: compression (tight ATR) and a fresh breakout are close to mutually exclusive on the same bar, since a breakout day naturally expands ATR. Abandoned once it was confirmed the base/compression/breakout criteria were visual judgment calls, not something reliable to encode as a formula.
P1 — percent-based trim, multi-day hold SMA200 + EMA + PMH/PML entry, -5% stop, trim 25% at +25% price move, another 25% at +50%, breakeven stop after each trim. Exact per-ticker figures weren't retained, but net P&L came in far below simple buy-and-hold on the same names despite genuine rallies in the underlying stocks — traced mainly to 20%-of-equity sizing diluting gains and to trim levels set as arbitrary price percentages rather than a risk-defined multiple. This directly motivated the switch to R-multiple-based trims and a fixed swing exit, i.e. Variant A.

Why it's the cleanest of the bunch: three independent breakout confirmations, gated to after 10am, is a much higher bar than "EMA stack + one PMH/PML break." By the time a stock clears premarket high, yesterday's high, and makes a fresh high-of-day, it has already proven real intraday strength — filtering out most of the false starts that hurt B–E. The cost of that selectivity: the EOD exit only captures the intraday leg, so absolute return stays modest despite the excellent quality metrics.

Why it's the best balance: giving F's stricter, higher-conviction entry room to run (2R trim + 20-day exit, instead of an EOD cutoff) lets winners compound further while keeping the false-positive rate low. DELL and MU roughly double A's return on the same names with far better profit factors (11.40 and 3.11 vs. A's 4.46 and 3.95), on fewer total trades (33 vs. 42) than A. The catch is sample size — the stricter entry cuts signal count sharply, and NVDA's 3-trade sample lands negative. NVDA has been the weakest or most-negative ticker in every variant tested this session, so this reads as a ticker-specific mismatch, not a flaw in G's logic.
Standing decision

Variant G is the current pick

Closest return to Variant A (+10.39% avg vs. A's +12.35%) while beating it on win rate (70.5% vs. 67.8%), profit factor (4.06 vs. 3.21), and total trades (33 vs. 42). It also inherits F's much cleaner entry logic — three stacked breakout confirmations instead of one — which is why 4 of 5 tickers show meaningfully better risk-adjusted numbers than under A alone.

Open item: NVDA's 3-trade sample under G is too thin to call decisively negative — worth revisiting once more history accumulates, rather than tuning the entry further on this sample size alone.
