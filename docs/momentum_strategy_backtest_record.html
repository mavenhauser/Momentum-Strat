Precursors (no per-ticker data retained)

P0 — pattern-based, abandoned Entry required a "big base near highs," ATR-based "compression," a "fresh breakout," market cap > $2B, and EMA8/21/50 all below price. Produced zero trades on AMD across the full history: compression (tight ATR) and a fresh breakout are close to mutually exclusive on the same bar, since a breakout day naturally expands ATR. Abandoned once it was confirmed the base/compression/breakout criteria were visual judgment calls, not something reliable to encode as a formula.
P1 — percent-based trim, multi-day hold SMA200 + EMA + PMH/PML entry, -5% stop, trim 25% at +25% price move, another 25% at +50%, breakeven stop after each trim. Exact per-ticker figures weren't retained, but net P&L came in far below simple buy-and-hold on the same names despite genuine rallies in the underlying stocks — traced mainly to 20%-of-equity sizing diluting gains and to trim levels set as arbitrary price percentages rather than a risk-defined multiple. This directly motivated the switch to R-multiple-based trims and a fixed swing exit, i.e. Variant A.

Downside stress test: does this depend on an exceptional bull run?

The direct test — rerunning H on the same 5 tickers over an earlier, rougher stretch — turned out to be impossible: TradingView's 10-minute intraday history for these symbols is hard-capped at Aug/Sep 2025 (confirmed via the Strategy Tester's internal dateRange.backtest metadata, identical across MU/AMD/NVDA/TSLA and nearly identical for DELL), and panning/scrolling the chart does not load anything earlier. Day-trade gap strategy (separate from Variant H, exploratory)

A distinct strategy concept, not a variant of H: same-day exit only, designed specifically around gap-day behavior (a stock can gap up premarket and still open weak and fade, or gap up and keep extending — the strategy needs to read which one is happening, not just assume continuation).

Attempt 1 — opening-range breakout/breakdown, rejected Gap filter (≥5% from prior close) + a ~20-minute opening range (9:30/9:40 bars on the 10-min chart) as the long/short trigger, stop at the opposite side of the range, forced flat at EOD. Tested on WULF, KLAC, AEHR (gap-ups) and RIVN, IONS, FRMI (gap-downs): 4 of 6 lost money, 2 produced zero trades. Two real problems found, not just weak performance: (1) the fade/reversal trade essentially never fired — every trade was a continuation in the original gap direction, meaning the setup never caught the "gaps up, opens weak, sells off" pattern it was built for; (2) RIVN (-11.17%) and IONS (-20.94%) — the two biggest reported gaps in the sample — produced zero trades, traced to a timing mismatch between the premarket scanner's snapshot-based gap % (captured before the bell) and the script's regular-session-open-based gap % (can diverge if the stock keeps moving between the premarket snapshot and 9:30).
Attempt 2 — "infused" into Variant H as a second entry pathway, reverted Kept H's swing-long entries exactly as-is, and added a new day-trade short pathway on top: any day with a ≥5% gap (either direction) where price then breaks below premarket low → short, stop at premarket high, forced flat at EOD. Tested on the 5 new tickers (AVGO, PLTR, MSFT, AAPL, GOOGL): the short pathway barely fired at all (1 trade out of 16 total, on PLTR) and the combined average (-0.20%) was worse than H alone. Reverted back to pure Variant H for two reasons: (1) the short pathway added complexity without adding much signal for this ticker set, and (2) a real logic flaw was identified — the same rule fires shorts on gap-downs that break even lower, which fights the well-known "gap fill" tendency (gap-downs statistically tend to bounce/mean-revert back toward prior close, not keep falling) rather than riding it.
Attempt 3 — gap-down long (mean-reversion), next up Directly motivated by the gap-fill critique above: instead of fighting the reversion tendency, trade with it. If a stock gaps down ≥5%, go long once the market opens (no confirmation trigger — first regular-session bar) and see if the bounce/fill has real edge. Exit: stop-loss + forced flat at EOD, consistent with the day-trade-only principle this whole thread started from. Not yet tested.

Premarket gappers scanner: low count diagnosis

Investigated why a scan day returned only 2 gappers. Confirmed via a raw re-fetch of the top 20 premarket movers by gap%: only 2 (FBRX $1.56B, TRAX $1.5B) cleared the scanner's $1B market cap filter — the other 18, including movers as large as +490%, were mostly microcap/nanocap names (several under $10M market cap). Stop-loss & trim redesign: option-primary, underlying as secondary filter only (2026-08-31)

Motivating case: NVDA's option premium dropped 54% (theta decay) while the underlying was barely down 2% — nowhere near the old -10% underlying-only stop, which had zero mechanism to see premium at all. Around the same time, the live trim's target-price leg was still an independent underlying-based trigger, not a "secondary filter" — inconsistent with the principle set this session: for an option position, the option's own premium is the primary basis for both stop and trim; the underlying only ever gates or confirms a premium-triggered decision, never triggers independently.

Three levels, each taking 25% of whatever remains at the time: +2R (+20%), +5R (+50%), +8R (+80%) — leaving a final ~25% of the original size to ride uncapped, still protected by the stop above and the time exit. The measured-move target_price from the 2026-08-15 work above stays alive, but demoted to a secondary confirmation noted in the first level's alert only ("target X also hit") — it no longer independently fires a trim on its own.

Contract selection: lower DTE floor, plus a "strong setup" weekly layer (2026-09-01)

Motivation: 45+ DTE contracts were both more expensive and structurally slow to react — low gamma at that much time value meant they barely responded to the kind of fast IV pop a real breakout produces ("no IV flush"). Two changes, not backtested (same judgment-call basis as the 2026-08-31 stop redesign — no historical option-premium data exists to test either against).

1. Primary contract: DTE floor lowered from 45 to 10 days

momentum_option_picker.pick_contract()'s selection logic didn't need to change — it already picks the nearest monthly OpEx clearing the min-DTE floor, falling back to the next monthly if that one fails the delta/volume/OI filters. Lowering MOMENTUM_TARGET_DTE_MIN to 10 just makes that resolve to "the nearest actual monthly OpEx" in practice: a signal today picks the Sep 18 contract; a signal next week still picks Sep 18 if there's enough runway left, otherwise rolls to Oct's OpEx automatically — exactly the worked example that motivated the change. The 10-day floor itself guarantees at least a week of room before the contract enters its own expiry week and gets catalyst-closed.

2. Weekly-layer add-on on a "strong" setup

"Strong" is defined as an intraday undercut-and-rally of the prior day's low, same day as Variant H's normal trigger: any bar's low trades below prev_day_low, followed by a bar (the same one or a later one) closing back at/above it — a classic same-day stop-hunt/shakeout-and-reclaim (check_undercut_and_rally() in momentum_signals.py). When both fire the same day, a second, much shorter-dated contract (2-9 calendar days out — a genuine weekly, picked by the new pick_weekly_contract(), same delta/volume/OI filters) is bought alongside the primary: 1% of NLV vs. the primary's 2%, appended as a second lot on the same ticker (the list-based state schema from BUG 6 already supports this), managed by the identical premium stop/trim ladder.

One rule needed a deliberate exception, caught before shipping rather than live: the existing "exit during the contract's own expiry week" catalyst rule assumes a monthly's 30-45+ day runway, where it rarely fires until late in the hold. A real weekly is inside its own expiry week from the moment it's bought — left unchanged, that rule would have force-closed every weekly layer on its very first management cycle after entry. Fixed two ways: the weekly layer is exempted from that specific check (is_weekly_layer flag on the position), and a tighter backstop takes its place — force-close once MOMENTUM_LAYER_EXPIRY_FLOOR_DAYS (1 day) from its own expiry, regardless of P&L, so it can never ride ungoverned all the way to actual physical expiration if neither the stop nor a trim level fires first. The theta>delta catalyst check still applies to it unchanged.

Verified against real IBKR/TastyTrade data before going live: the DTE-window candidate lists resolve exactly as expected (primary → Sep 18 / Oct 16 monthly OpEx; layer → Sep 4 / Sep 9 weeklies, checked against NVDA's real chain), and a dry-run pass across all 4 open lots plus the full 10-ticker universe raised no exceptions with the new is_weekly_layer field (defaults to false via .get() on every pre-existing position).

Qullamaggie research pass: breakeven-after-first-trim wired in (2026-09-01)

Researched Kristjan Kullamägi's ("Qullamaggie") public trading rules to check for anything worth borrowing beyond the undercut-and-rally pattern already in use. His three core setups are Breakout (prior 30-100%+ move, 2-8 week orderly pullback surfing the 10/20-day MA, then a fresh breakout), Episodic Pivot (10%+ gap on a news/volume catalyst, bought near the opening-range high), and Undercut & Rally. The transferable substance is in his trade management: sell 1/3-1/2 of the position at 2-3R or after 3-5 days, move the stop to breakeven, then trail the remainder with a close below the 10-day or 20-day MA (an intraday wick through the MA doesn't count, only a closing violation).

Found a dead field while checking this against our own code: breakeven_active has existed in the state schema since the 2026-08-31 stop/trim redesign and gets set True by _trim_one_quarter() on every first trim - but nothing ever read it. The stop stayed at -25% premium on the remaining size even after a trim had already locked in real gains, which isn't what that field's own name implies. Fixed directly: manage_position() now picks the stop's R threshold based on breakeven_active - MOMENTUM_PREMIUM_STOP_R (-25%) before the first trim, MOMENTUM_BREAKEVEN_STOP_R (0R, i.e. entry premium) after it. The EMA/LoD gate is unchanged and still applies to both thresholds equally, so a normal pullback still isn't sold into just because the stop tightened.

Episodic Pivot and ADR-scaled position sizing were also identified as plausible future additions but not pursued here - EP would be a genuinely separate entry signal (news-gap + volume) rather than a refinement of Variant H, and ADR-scaling is a bigger sizing-model change than a single config tweak.

Time exit removed from live code (2026-09-01)

Prompted by a live edge case: NVDA sat at exactly 20 trading days held and 1.45% OTM ($226.71 vs. a $230 strike) - close enough that the binary ITM/OTM check at the exact deadline was about to force a close on a position that hadn't actually failed, just hadn't crossed the strike yet by a trivial margin. Traced the rule's origin: it's not something invented for the options system - it's Variant A's "close at 20 trading days" (chosen because "a 20-day hold is long enough for a real breakout to clear its 2R target without drifting back into chop"), carried forward unchanged through G and H, and deliberately left in place during the 2026-08-31 option-primary redesign.

Decided to remove it, not patch around the edge case, because the backtest evidence for keeping it doesn't hold up under scrutiny. Its calibration (20 days, single 2R trim-to-breakeven) predates the current live design entirely - three levels of premium-based trim (2R/5R/8R) plus a premium stop that now also tightens to breakeven after the first trim (see above). Nothing in this doc has re-tested 20 days against that combination. The one backtest that removed a time exit at all, Variant I ("trend-ride" exit, rejected), confounded the comparison by also removing the 2R trim in the same test - its own verdict attributes the underperformance to losing the trim's downside protection, not to removing the time exit specifically. So there's no clean evidence either way that a hold-duration exit contributes anything on top of what the stop/catalyst/trim ladder already does; it's inherited scaffolding from an earlier, simpler exit design, not a re-validated rule.

Removed manage_position()'s time-exit/ITM-extension block entirely (previously step 4) along with the now-fully-dead MOMENTUM_ITM_EXTENSION_TRADING_DAYS config constant, the itm_extension_deadline state field, and the add_trading_days() helper. MOMENTUM_TIME_EXIT_TRADING_DAYS and trading_days_since() are kept, marked backtest-only, since scripts/momentum_target_price_backtest.py still uses them and that backtest's results are already recorded in this doc. Exits are now purely: premium stop (gated by the EMA/LoD underlying check) → catalyst (theta>delta or expiry week) → trim ladder. Verified live: NVDA now returns still_open=True with no close attempt in a dry-run pass against real data, where it previously would have closed on schedule.
