Day Trading Setup

Three alert-only TradingView indicators for intraday opening-range breakout trades: wait for a stock to break a key level, let it pull back to the 8 EMA or the broken level, and enter when a candle closes back above the 8 EMA. This page records what each indicator does, how to trade with them, and what was built and fixed to get them running on the Day Trading Set Up layout. Separate from the Momentum Strategy (Variant H) system and shares no code with it.

How a trade comes together

Screener ▲ row · DTS Screener→A / A+ OUTSIDE LONG · banner→CLEAN UPTREND + above VWAP · panel ↳▲ breakout marker · DTS Trend→armed→◆ retest→LONG label: entry / stop / target · DTS Entry ↳2R ✓ stop → BE→TRAIL (close below 8 EMA)orEOD 15:55 ↳STOP · low hits the stop before 2R

Each step is something you can see on the chart. Nothing fires mid-candle: every signal waits for the 5-minute bar to close.

The layout

The layout is named Day Trading Set Up in TradingView. The three DTS scripts draw no EMA, VWAP or pivot lines of their own. Built-in indicators handle those, and DTS Trend and DTS Entry calculate the same 8 / 21 / 50 EMAs internally for their rules.

DTS 2

DTS Trend: is the trend clean, and which levels matter

Clean trend8 > 21 > 50, spread ≥ 0.10 ATR, 21 EMA rising ≥ 0.05 ATR over 3 bars
Alerts5 named conditions
  • Trend ribbon: the band between the 8 and 21 EMAs is green in a clean uptrend, red in a clean downtrend and purple in chop.
  • Key levels: PDH / PDL (prior regular session, solid), PMH / PML (premarket, dotted), ORH / ORL (opening range, yellow, first 5 minutes), PWH / PWL (prior week, thick). Each gets a flag tag at the right edge.
  • Breakout markers: ▲ ORH / PMH / PDH / PWH below a candle that closed above a level, ▼ for the lows. Bright in a clean trend, gray in chop. Gray means skip it.
  • Market structure: confirmed swings labelled HH / LH / HL / LL, with a dotted BOS line when a close breaks the last swing.
  • Bar coloring: off, so candles keep their normal red and green. "Trend colors" paints every candle by the trend instead, which makes down candles green in an uptrend.
DTS 3

DTS Entry: breakout, retest, entry

Entryclose back above the 8 EMA after a retest
Stopretest low − 0.05 ATR
Target2R, then trail the 8 EMA
  1. Breakout (arm). In the regular session, a candle closes above ORH, PMH, PDH or PWH with close > 8 EMA > 21 EMA. If several levels break on one bar, the highest is used.
  2. Disarm. Cancelled by a close below the 21 EMA, or a close more than 0.25 ATR back below the broken level.
  3. Retest. A later bar's low comes within 0.15 ATR of the 8 EMA or the broken level. A ◆ diamond appears.
  4. Entry. A bar closes green, above the 8 EMA but no more than 0.75 ATR above it, still above the level, in a clean trend, above VWAP, inside 09:35–12:00, with no open trade and fewer than 3 entries today.
  5. Exit. Stop, 2R target with stop to break-even, then a close below the 8 EMA, or flat at 15:55.

How to use it

1. Before 09:30: pick the names (extended hours on)

  • Click RTH at the bottom right of the chart so it reads ETH. The screener and today's levels are then live.
  • The screener's list is refreshed for you at 08:30 and again at 09:15 New York time.
  • Longs: ▲ rows at the top of the screener. Shorts: ▼ rows at the bottom. Skip ⚠ rows or keep them on watch.
  • Load your top one or two names.

2. At 09:30: switch back to RTH and check the backdrop

  • Banner: A+ / A OUTSIDE LONG is the setup you want. B / C is weaker. INSIDE RANGE means stand aside.
  • Panel → Trend must read CLEAN UPTREND. On CHOP the entry rules won't fire anyway.
  • Panel → VWAP must say above.

3. Watch the entry sequence

StepWhat you'll seeMeaning
Breakout▲ ORH / PMH / PDH / PWH below a candleA candle closed above a key level. Bright green = clean trend. Gray = chop, ignore it.
ArmedPanel Long row: armed ORH 119.18 → wait retestThe setup is live. It cancels on a close below the 21 EMA or a failed breakout.
Retest◆ green diamondA pullback tagged the 8 EMA or the broken level. Get ready.
EntryGreen LONG label with Entry / Stop / Target and three linesThe candle closed back above the 8 EMA with every filter passing. Enter at or just after that close.

4. Manage the trade

  • 2R ✓ stop → BE: take a partial and move your stop to your entry.
  • TRAIL: the rest exits when price gives back 40% of its best move since entry, on a 5-minute close (since v8.0; it stands in for trailing the option's own price). BE if price comes back to your entry first.
  • STOP: the low hit the stop before 2R.
  • EOD: flat by 15:55.

When to skip

  • Gray breakout markers, a CHOP panel, or the INSIDE RANGE banner.
  • MIDDAY - CAUTION (11:30–14:00), even though the entry window runs to 12:00.
  • A breakout that has run far from the 8 EMA with no pullback. The script won't chase it.

Premarket vs regular hours

The scripts only update when a new bar prints on the chart. With extended hours off, the newest bar before the open is yesterday's last candle, so everything is one session behind until 09:30.

RTH (extended off) Screener shows yesterday's close. PDH / PDL are from two sessions ago. Banner reads CLOSED 100%. EMAs and VWAP are regular-session only. VWAP starts at 09:30. Use this to trade.

Alerts

20 alerts are live (set up 2026-10-12): one DTS Entry · TICKER alert for each of the 20 screener tickers. Each sends entries (with entry, stop and target), new A+ setups, the 2R target hit and every exit (stop, break-even, trail, end of day). They notify by phone app push and on-screen pop-up, with no sound, and never expire. There is no alert for screener flips.

  • They follow the daily list. Each morning's run deletes the alerts of the tickers that left the list and creates alerts for the new ones. The 12 fixed tickers keep theirs.
  • They stay current. An alert that has stopped, or was made with an older script version or different settings, is replaced on the next run.
  • What they send follows the chart. DTS Entry has a switch for A+ messages and one for 2R and exit messages, both on. Change a switch on the chart and the next run replaces the alerts to match.
  • No screener-flip alert and no sound. In the first hour the original setup fired 29 times with a tone each time, 7 of them screener flips, so the flip alert and the sound were removed.
  • Only alerts named DTS Entry · … are managed. Anything else on the account is left alone.

A message looks like this: DTS: LONG NVDA @ 177.16 | stop 176.40 | target 178.68 (2R) | ORH breakout + 8 EMA retest | grade A+ OUTSIDE. The grade (added 2026-10-16) sets the trade size.

Acting on the signals: IBKR executor

Since 2026-10-14 every DTS Entry alert also goes to a small program on this Mac that trades weekly options on the Interactive Brokers paper account. TradingView decides every entry, stop, target and exit. IBKR's prices are only used to fill the option orders.

Sent only once price breaks the trigger bar's high (long) / low (short) within the next three 5-minute bars, 15 minutes (since v9.0; 15 minutes since v10.0); unconfirmed triggers lapse. On Oct 14–19 this kept 40 of 52 signals, +27.6R vs +16.1R. Buys a call / a put. Expiry: this week's (normally Friday, 0DTE on Friday itself); on expiry day the same-day option is bought until 12:00 ET; afternoon entries buy next week's. Strike: never in the money. Start at the at-the-money strike (one in the money by at most 0.10% of the price counts as at the money) and step out of the money until one buys 4 contracts within the budget, never below 0.15 delta (weeklies, so further out of the money is fine); failing that, the nearest that buys 2; failing that, 1 contract, held through 2R. Every strike must pass the liquidity check (two-sided quote, mid price at least $0.50, spread ≤ 15% of mid, open interest ≥ 100, at least 100 contracts traded today).   • Orders: limit at mid, priced off TastyTrade's live quote (IBKR's option data on this account is 15 minutes delayed). Anything unfilled after 30 seconds is retried once at the ask or bid. The real IBKR position is read before every sale.
  • Limits: one position per ticker, at most 10 open, paper account only. A 15:56 sweep closes options expiring that day.
  • Telegram: every action is sent with the key facts in bold (action, contract, CALL / PUT, price, P&L) in separate paragraphs. A warning is sent if TWS stops accepting connections, checked every 5 minutes during market hours.

What the day showed

  • Two halves. 10:20–11:00 was mostly one-bar stop-outs and tiny targets. After 11:00 the short setups trended, and every trade that reached 2R and then trailed made money. NVDA, DELL and MSFT made most of the profit.
  • Mostly one bet. 10 of 14 trades were shorts on tech names on a day tech fell. One trending day is not evidence that the setup works.
  • Tight stops don't suit options. Several signals had stops and targets of a few tenths of a percent, smaller than the option spread plus the gap between the target touch and the bar-close fill. Two trades TradingView scored as 2R winners (JNJ, AMZN early) lost money at that stage.
  • A "gain at target ≥ 2× spread" filter would have skipped only the two JNJ trades (about +$1,760 better). The quick stop-outs (TSLA, CVX, AVGO) need a minimum stop distance instead.
  • The 5-position cap skipped 12 signals and filled with correlated short-tech trades. One more AMD entry was lost to an option-chain timeout.

Lessons at true prices (Oct 14–16, 33 trades)

On Oct 16 it turned out the executor's option quotes were the 15-minute-delayed TastyTrade demo feed. Every fill was re-priced from 1-minute option history (realistic: true price ± half the spread). The figures below replace the earlier ones. Returns are per dollar of premium, so days with different sizes compare fairly.

  1. The setup works when it reaches 2R. 17 trades reached 2R: 16 made money, averaging +65% of premium. The 15 that stopped out first averaged −31%. The trailed half makes most of it (CRWD 232.5P +308%, MSFT 535P +294%, META +218% on Oct 16).
  2. Tiny stops lose. Stops under 0.20% of price: 8 trades, 25% winners, −8% average. At 0.20% or more: 25 trades, 60% winners, +37% average. The 0.20% filter stays.
  3. The first hour is the weak spot, on every day. Entries before 10:30 ET: 8 trades, 2 winners, −25% average. 10:30–12:00: +47%. From 12:00: +32%. Passing the stop filter and entering from 10:30 on: 18 trades, 72% winners, +60% average.
  4. The JNJ "spread ate the win" lesson was the delayed quotes. At true prices JNJ #2 made money. The 2× spread filter is kept as a sanity check, not as a proven edge.
  5. Very cheap options are lottery tickets. KO 94C at $0.05 (277 contracts, delta 0.21) lost 90%. One trade, but worth a minimum option price.
  6. Trend days carry it. Oct 14 (down day) and Oct 16 (tech selling off after 10:30) made the money; Oct 15 (reversal) was −$450 at true prices, mostly small stop-outs.
  7. Infrastructure. Keep the Mac awake (now automatic 09:10–16:10 ET) and get real-time option quotes before paper orders: the dry run can be re-priced after the fact, live orders cannot.

Three days is a small sample, two of them strong trend days. Treat every number here as a hypothesis to keep testing.

DTS Trend's default "Trend colors" painted every candle by the trend, so down candles were green in an uptrend.
Fix
Bar coloring set to Off on the chart and made the default in the script (2026-10-03). The trend still shows in the ribbon and the panel.
FIX-5

DTS Trend overwritten by another workstream

Symptom
On 2026-10-03 the SPX Scalping workstream saved its own 69-line script over DTS Trend twice (versions 4.0 and 6.0). Same tool flaw as FIX-3.
Fix
It was restored within about a minute each time (5.0 and 7.0). Version 7.0 was verified identical to the repo, and the chart's settings were intact. Sessions on this project now confirm the editor title before pasting.
FIX-6

Orders priced off delayed IBKR quotes

Symptom
The first live entry (TSLA put) used IBKR's quote, 6.75/6.90, while the live market was 6.85/7.00. IBKR returned error 10091: no real-time options data for the API on this account, so quotes are 15 minutes delayed.
Fix
Orders are priced off TastyTrade's live quote first. IBKR is a fallback only, labelled as possibly delayed.
FIX-7

AMD entry lost to a chain timeout

Symptom
AMD's option chain (over 7,000 contracts) took about 15 seconds, the shared client's limit, so the entry failed with an empty error message.
Fix
Chain downloads allow 60 seconds with one retry, exits quote the stored contract without downloading the chain, and error messages name the error type.
FIX-8

TWS dropped unnoticed

Symptom
TWS stopped accepting connections at 11:45 ET, and nothing said so. Four entries were sized off the $100k placeholder.
Fix
A Telegram warning on the first failure and a note on reconnect, plus a check of the TWS port every 5 minutes during market hours.

The executor skips an entry when TradingView's stop is less than 0.20% of the price from the entry, and when no strike's expected gain at the 2R target (|delta| × the move) is at least 2× its bid/ask spread. On the Oct 14–15 trades these would have skipped 9 trades worth −$2,482. They were chosen from only 24 trades, so they are a starting point, not a tested edge.

Limits of the tool

The screener makes one data request per ticker, 20 at most, against TradingView's limit of 40. PMH / PML stay blank on symbols with no premarket session. The layout uses 8 indicators, which needs a paid TradingView plan.

Where it stands

The tooling works. The edge is unproven.

The signals now drive a dry-run options executor on the IBKR paper account; its first session ended about +$11,300 at realistic fills on a strongly trending day. All three indicators compile, run and agree with TradingView's own VWAP and levels. The layout is saved on a 5-minute chart, and the daily routine is ETH before 09:30 to pick names, RTH from 09:30 to trade. Take entries only when the banner reads A or A+ OUTSIDE, the panel reads a clean trend, and the LONG or SHORT label prints on a closed candle.

Nothing here has been backtested. Treat every signal as a prompt to look, and size it as a paper trade until you have your own record of how the setup performs.
